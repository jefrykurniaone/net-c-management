# Every member Session surface draws its own card, and what stays shared is the two resolvers and four card conventions

ADR 0003 retired the one shared Slot Cell, and that is not re-argued here. What it left open is the thing this record settles: if the week strip, the dashboard and the session detail page each compose their own card markup, what stops the three drifting into three different products. The answer has two halves. The *deciding* is shared and the *drawing* is not — `resolveSessionStanding` answers what state a Session is in and `slotActionFor` answers which action, if any, a member may take on it, both resolved server-side into view data before any component is reached, so nothing inside a card works out a colour, a label or a permission. And the card layout follows four conventions, settled by `WeekSessionCard` (#159) because it was the first of them and mirrored since by the dashboard card (#160), the detail header (#161) and the pay surface (#162).

**The four conventions.** First, information order, top to bottom, always: start–end time as Figure; the Activity tile beside the Session title; the venue as Caption; at most one note under it; then the footer. Second, the chip sits at the footer's leading edge — one chip, never two, and the free-Seat figure stands in its place where the standing is a number. Third, the action sits at the footer's trailing edge and is a **sibling** of the card's link, never a child of it: a control nested inside a link is invalid markup, is unreachable in the tab order some of the time, and is activated differently by different browsers, so the card link and the action are two tab stops in that order. Fourth, the card stacks and never puts a chip beside a figure on one line, because a day column is about 174px wide (`STRIP_MEASURE`) and the longest chip label this product sets is most of it. The whole information area is the link; the footer is not, because it holds a state rather than a destination and a button.

**Each surface's deviations are deliberate, and are the reason the cards are separate files at all.** The dashboard card adds the Session's own date as a Label-role prefix on the time line, because unlike a week-strip card it carries no day column above it to say which day it is. The session detail header carries no action row: the RSVP card further down that page is what claims or withdraws a Seat, and a second control on the header would answer the same question twice. That header is also not passed `holdExpiresAt`, so a Seat held on money nobody has verified yet reads there as a plain Registered chip, exactly as the retired Slot Cell's own resolution read it; the RSVP card below is where the payment deadline is surfaced. Changing that would be a behaviour change, and the restyle that split these cards deliberately did not make it.

**Two surfaces choose different sets of days, and for opposite reasons.** The week strip draws every day of its range, empty ones included — a day with nothing on it keeps its column and draws a dashed empty slot, because skipping it turns the week into a short list and hides the one thing an Admin most needs members to notice, that nothing has been posted. The days come from the shared day-range builder, which yields one entry per day of the range and never drops one. The dashboard's Activity body does the opposite: it flattens the same range into one ordered list of cards, and only a day with a posted Session or a standing slot becomes a card, because that surface is not a week — it answers "what is next for this Activity", and an Activity with nothing at all across the range yields an empty list the card draws as one neutral-chipped sentence rather than a card per empty day. Consistently with ADR 0003, the module that resolves the dashboard's cards is a *sibling* of the week strip's, not a caller of it: each surface's view is as much its own as its drawing.

This record decides nothing about what a Session is, who may claim a Seat, or how capacity is counted; those live in the payment and seat-holding rules the server enforces under a row lock. It decides only what a member-facing Session card looks like, what a component drawing one is allowed to work out for itself, and which departures from the four conventions are sanctioned.

Status: accepted, 2026-09-04.
